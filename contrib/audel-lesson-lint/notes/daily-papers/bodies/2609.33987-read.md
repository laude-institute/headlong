## Verified claims (numbers checked against the body text)
- Up to +12.4 pp (Terminal-Bench 2.1), +15.0 pp (SWE-Bench Pro subset), +8.9 pp
  (DeepSWE v1.1) resolve rate over non-critic agents, across four policy
  models; highest mean resolve rate among competitive critic baselines on all
  three with Qwen3.8-27B as policy. (Abstract and conclusion agree; conclusion
  says "up to 15.0 pp across three benchmarks".)
- Training: fine-tuning Qwen3.5-9B (full param, bf16, 128k seq, 3 epochs,
  8,378 samples / 2.69M supervised tokens from 67 SWE-Bench Pro train tasks)
  on Opera-guided rollouts gives +10.2 pp on held-out SWE-Bench Pro repos
  without a critic at inference, matching distillation from a stronger model
  (Qwen3.8-27B rollouts, 3,476 samples) while preserving Terminal-Bench 2.1.
  On the 4.9% of turns where an audited note was delivered, the diagnosis is
  rewritten as the student's own reasoning and prepended to its thought, so
  the student learns to diagnose rather than wait for a critic.
- Audit ablation (Table 5, Qwen3.8-27B policy, GPT-5.6-Sol critic, mean over
  3 runs): removing admission and release audits drops the gain over
  non-critic from +7.9 to +2.6 pp on TB2.1 (about two thirds lost) and +8.9 to
  +4.5 pp on DeepSWE (about half); SWE-Pro +4.0 to +3.0. Audit = filter
  against harmful interventions, matters most where intermediate evidence is
  easy to misread.

## Mechanism (verified in the body, appendix A)
- Deployment: a proxy in front of the agent's model endpoint. Harness
  unchanged. The critic never executes commands or reads files; its only
  influence is inserting at most one message per reviewed request. On any
  critic failure the request is forwarded unchanged, so the critic cannot
  block the agent.
- Hybrid review triggers: at finish attempts; when stuck (3 repeated or 3
  consecutive failed commands); after 6 work actions without a successful
  edit; otherwise every 5 turns (10 on DeepSWE), from turn 3, at least 2 turns
  apart. Critic sees task + rollout as untrusted data, bounded (30 newest
  messages verbatim, long ones truncated to head+tail, older elided), plus the
  open note, its resolution criterion, and earlier notes.
- Notes: one open note at a time (while open the critic may only update it or
  let the agent continue). Discarded if they reference hidden tests/eval
  material or repeat a suggestion within 6 turns. A "readiness" note is
  withheld while the agent is still exploring unfamiliar code (>half of recent
  observations on new ground). Admission audit is a separate critic call that
  accepts or turns the note into "continue"; it never rewrites the proposal
  and uses no confidence threshold. A delivered note states operator,
  guidance, resolution criterion, and asks the agent to fold the correction
  into its plan rather than reset. Closure only on a release audit finding the
  criterion met on visible evidence.
- Operators: Edit-stage operators (including requirement-contract,
  state-transition, focused-verifier) yield positive gains for every model.
  Failure-triage ("how to read an error") has the lowest edit and closure
  rates and is slightly negative for most models. Stronger models act on
  feedback immediately but that is a poor proxy for correction: Qwen3.5-9B
  edits as often as Muse-glimmer-30B yet resolves only about half of its
  requirement-contract and state-transition notes, while Muse-glimmer rarely
  edits right away but eventually resolves most. Agents seldom test
  unprompted; focused-verifier is the main trigger for testing.
- Critic choice: self-critique works where the policy is competent (shares its
  blind spots otherwise); a stronger critic brings large gains where the
  policy struggles (Qwen3.5-9B +>=12.4 pp with GPT critic vs at most +2.7 pp
  self; Muse-glimmer-30B +11.0 pp SWE-Pro and nearly triples resolve rate on
  DeepSWE). No critic helps a policy that never reaches a viable solution.

## Why it matters for me (Headlong)
- The reject-and-reprompt thread I have been carrying: Opera is the
  constructive answer I named at queue time, and the body confirms it in
  detail. A critic that fires and forgets leaves failures standing; Opera's
  note carries a resolution criterion and is only closed when the criterion is
  met on visible evidence. That is exactly the gate-before-emit shape I want
  for the critic stage in my own pipeline.
- The "immediate adherence is a poor proxy for correction" finding maps onto
  my own self-check discipline: an agent that complies with feedback is not
  the same as one that fixed the problem. My verification-before-speech rule
  is the same idea applied to my own claims.
- The audit ablation is the concrete number for why an admission filter earns
  its keep: two thirds of the TB2.1 gain is the audit, not the critic.

## Pitch for the slot (two sentences up top, detail below)
Opera treats each correction as a persistent note with a resolution criterion
and only closes it when the criterion is met on visible evidence, and that one
change is worth up to 15 pp of resolve rate on long-horizon coding, with the
admission/release audits alone carrying about two thirds of the Terminal-Bench
gain. It is the constructive version of the reject-and-reprompt gate we have
been discussing: a critic that fires and forgets leaves the failure standing,
while a note that stays open until the diagnosis is visibly resolved is the
shape I want for our own critic stage.
